# Replacing the synthetic corpora with real data

Every dataset the models train on today is generated by scripts in `ml/`. The
generators are principled — they encode real Bangladeshi epidemiology, seasonal
patterns and clinical relationships — but no row came from a patient. That is
the single largest limitation of the system and the precondition stated in
every model card.

This document records what real data each component needs, what is already
obtainable, and what requires an institution to grant access.

Availability was checked by downloading each source, not by reading its
description. Row counts below are what actually arrived.

---

## 1. Symptom text → urgency (the triage classifier)

**Currently:** 9,000 generated sentences (`ml/generate_triage_dataset.py`).

**What a real row needs**

| Field | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `text` | "তিন দিন ধরে জ্বর, শরীর ব্যথা" | Exactly as the patient wrote or said it |
| `language` | bn / bl / en / mixed | Bangla, romanised, English, code-switched |
| `age_years` | 34 | Arrives as a form field, not in the text |
| `sex` | F | Changes the differential |
| `duration_days` | 3 | Separates flu from tuberculosis |
| `temperature_c` | 38.5 | Optional but decisive when present |
| `triage_level` | 1–5 | **Assigned by a clinician, not by a rule** |
| `final_diagnosis` | dengue | Confirmed later, where known |

The label is the hard part. Text can be gathered; a defensible urgency label
requires a doctor to read each case. Roughly 3,000–5,000 clinician-labelled
rows would allow the synthetic corpus to be retired.

**What exists**

| Source | Contents | Licence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Bangla Disease-Symptom, Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/rjgjh8hgrt/5) | 758 rows, 172 Bangla symptom names, 85 diseases | CC BY 4.0 | **Downloaded and inspected.** Structured symptom→disease pairs, not free text and no urgency label. Directly useful for widening the lexicon. |
| [Bangla-MedER, Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/jt4gywvwtj/2) | 2,980 annotated Bangla medical texts | CC BY 4.0 | Entity spans for symptom, disease, drug. Useful for extraction, not for triage. |
| [BanglaBioMed](https://aclanthology.org/2022.bionlp-1.31/) | 12,000 tokens, IOB format | Research | Same: entities, no urgency. |
| [Bangla Medical Entity, Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/tanjimtaharataurpa/bangla-medical-entity-dataset) | 6,895 Bangla medical statements | Kaggle terms | Closest to real patient phrasing found so far. |

**Verdict on Bangla:** no public dataset carries a clinician-assigned urgency
label for Bangla free text. That has to be collected locally — see the
collection protocol at the end of this document.

### English sources with a real clinician triage label

Bangla is the blocker for *phrasing*, not for *how urgency relates to
presentation*. That relationship is clinical, not linguistic: chest pain with
sweating is an emergency in any language. English ED datasets carry exactly the
label we cannot get in Bangla, and several are open.

| Source | Contents | Access | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| [NHAMCS (US CDC)](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Datasets/NHAMCS/) | National ED survey. Per visit: 5-level triage immediacy (`IMMEDR`), up to 3 coded reasons for visit, full vitals, age, sex, arrival mode, diagnoses, disposition | **Fully open. No registration, no DUA.** | **Downloaded and parsed.** 2022 file: 16,025 visits, **10,207 with a triage level 1–5**. Files published for 2011–2022. |
| [MIMIC-IV-ED](https://physionet.org/content/mimic-iv-ed/2.2/) | 425,000 ED stays. `acuity` (ESI 1–5) assigned by the triage nurse plus **`chiefcomplaint` as free text** | Credentialed: PhysioNet account + CITI training + DUA | The single best match — free-text complaint *and* a nurse-assigned ESI. Free, but approval takes days to weeks. |
| [MIETIC](https://physionet.org/content/mietic/1.0.0/) | 9,629 structured triage cases from MIMIC-IV, ESI-aligned | Same credentialing | Pre-cleaned; saves the extraction work |
| [Korean KTAS, Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ilkeryildiz/emergency-service-triage-application) | 1,267 records, chief complaint text, vitals, **KTAS level validated by three triage experts** | Open | Small but expert-labelled |
| [Iran ED, Mendeley](https://doi.org/10.17632/vhzyyktrz5.1) | 143,582 ED stays, chief complaint (ICD-10 coded), vitals, triage grade | Open, CC licence | Large; non-Western case mix, closer to Bangladesh |

**NHAMCS, verified by download.** The 2022 file gives this real distribution —
note it matches what any ED sees, with level 3 dominating:

| Triage level | Visits | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Immediate | 139 | 1.4% |
| 2 Emergent | 1,595 | 15.6% |
| 3 Urgent | 5,340 | 52.3% |
| 4 Semi-urgent | 2,826 | 27.7% |
| 5 Non-urgent | 307 | 3.0% |

Our generated corpus is close to uniform across the five levels. Real triage is
not: over half of all arrivals are level 3. Training on a uniform corpus teaches
the model a prior that does not exist in any emergency department.

**How English data would be used.** Not as Bangla training text — translation
would produce stilted phrasing no patient uses. Two honest uses:

1. **Calibrate the severity prior.** Reweight the synthetic corpus so the level
   distribution matches NHAMCS rather than being uniform.
2. **Validate the rules.** Check the red-flag rules and condition weights
   against tens of thousands of real presentations: does "chest pain + sweating"
   actually carry the acuity our rules assign it?

The Bangla surface forms still have to come from Bangladeshi patients. What
English data supplies is the clinical relationship underneath, at a scale no
local collection will reach for years.

## 2. Bed occupancy → surge forecast

**Currently:** 7,200 generated rows, 5 hospitals, 2 years.

**What a real row needs**

```
date, hospital_id, ward_type, capacity, occupied
```

Daily, per ward, for at least 18 months so the model sees a full dengue season
twice. Admissions and discharges per day would improve it further.

**What exists**

| Source | Contents | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| [DGHS Hospital Services dashboard](https://dashboard.dghs.gov.bd/pages/dashboard_hospital_services.php) | Bed occupancy rate by facility type, monthly | Real and current, but aggregated — no daily per-ward series |
| [DGHS Health Bulletin (annual)](https://old.dghs.gov.bd/index.php/en/publications/health-bulletin/dghs-health-bulletin) | Beds, admissions, deaths, occupancy per facility | Per facility but yearly, and published as PDF tables |
| [DGHS facility scoring pages](https://dashboard.dghs.gov.bd/pages/hss_scoring_facility_detail.php) | Monthly bed occupancy per named facility | Machine-readable per facility, monthly granularity |

**Verdict:** DGHS already publishes this, but monthly and per facility rather
than daily and per ward. Monthly national data can validate the model's
seasonal shape; daily ward-level data must come from a hospital's own admission
system under a data-sharing agreement.

## 3. Disease surveillance

**Currently:** 15,072 generated rows, 12 districts, 8 diseases.

**What exists — this one is solved.**

| Source | Contents | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| [DENV-Data-Analysis (GitHub)](https://github.com/awnonbhowmik/DENV-Data-Analysis) | Dengue 2001–2024: monthly infections, deaths, division-level counts, temperature, rainfall, humidity | **Downloaded and inspected.** 289 monthly rows plus division breakdown. Directly usable. |
| [Dengue + Weather 2022–2025, Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/brownsugar297/dengue-and-weather-data-bangladesh-20222025) | 11,088 daily rows, all 8 divisions, with weather | CC0. Daily granularity — better than what we generate. |
| [DGHS daily dengue bulletin](https://old.dghs.gov.bd/index.php/bd/home/5200-daily-dengue-status-report) | The official source both of the above derive from | Primary source, published daily |
| [IEDCR](https://iedcr.gov.bd/) | National surveillance for other notifiable diseases | Cross-check source |

**Verdict:** real dengue data is freely available at daily resolution and
should replace the generated surveillance corpus. Other diseases are patchier;
IEDCR publishes them less consistently.

## 4. Hospital and pharmacy directory

**Currently:** 5 hand-written hospitals, 6 pharmacies, 4 labs in `data/seed/`.

**What exists — this one is solved.**

[Bangladesh Healthsites (HDX / OpenStreetMap)](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/bangladesh-healthsites) —
ODbL licence, downloaded and counted:

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Total facilities | 7,280 |
| With a name | 6,743 |
| With GPS coordinates | 6,172 |
| **Hospitals with coordinates** | **1,563** |
| Pharmacies | 3,287 |
| Clinics | 796 |
| Diagnostic laboratories | 42 |

Columns include `name`, `X`/`Y` (lon/lat), `amenity`, `healthcare`, `beds`,
`emergency`, `operator`, `opening_hours`, `speciality`.

Bed counts are almost entirely missing (2 of 1,563) and emergency is flagged on
only 92, so those still need a survey or DGHS data. But the **names, types and
coordinates are real**, which is what the proximity ranking depends on.

Other options: [LGED health facilities](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/bangladesh-health-facilities-by-lged) (shapefile),
[Cox's Bazar registry](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/health-facility-registry) (CC0, camp facilities only).

## 5. Drug brand → generic and interactions

**Currently:** 152 brand aliases → 73 generics, 81 interaction pairs.

**What exists**

| Source | Contents | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| [DGDA Generic Drug Database](https://www.dgdagov.info/index.php/search-price) | Official register: generic, strength, pack size, **retail price**, manufacturer, brand, indication | The authoritative source. Has an Excel export. Table loads via JavaScript, so it needs a browser session or a written request to DGDA. |
| [Medicinal Products in Bangladesh, Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/zhtvkny53n/1) | Generic and brand names, strengths, manufacturers | CC BY. Ready to use. |
| [All Medicine Data of Bangladesh, Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/general/311821) | 25,000+ medicines with price, generic, indication, drug class | Kaggle terms |
| [DrugBangladesh](https://drugbangladesh.com/) | 30,000+ brands, 1,500+ generics, 300+ companies | Bilingual site; scraping needs permission |
| [National Essential Drugs List (WHO mirror)](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/searo/hsd/edm/neml-ban-2008-govweb-ok.pdf) | 209 essential drugs | Public, but last revised 2008 |

Interaction pairs are not Bangladesh-specific and should come from a
pharmacological reference rather than a local list. The current 81 pairs cover
the common dangerous combinations but are not exhaustive.

**Verdict:** brand→generic mapping can be replaced with real data immediately.
Prices are available from DGDA and would make the pharmacy search far more
useful than the current synthetic prices.

## Summary

| Component | Real data available? | Blocker |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital/pharmacy directory | **Yes — 1,563 hospitals, 3,287 pharmacies with GPS** | None. Licence attribution only. |
| Dengue surveillance | **Yes — daily, 2001–2025** | None. |
| Drug brand→generic | **Yes — several sources, 25k+ entries** | Licence check; DGDA export needs a session |
| Bangla symptom vocabulary | **Partly — 172 symptoms, 85 diseases** | Structured pairs, not free text |
| Bed occupancy (daily, per ward) | Aggregated only | Needs a hospital data-sharing agreement |
| Triage label ↔ presentation (English) | **Yes — 10,207 rows verified, no registration** | None for NHAMCS; PhysioNet needs CITI training |
| **Bangla symptom text with a clinician urgency label** | **No** | **Must be collected. Ethics approval + clinical partner.** |

The first four can be done now and would remove most of the synthetic data from
the system. English ED data additionally fixes the severity prior, which is
currently uniform and therefore wrong.

The last one cannot be bought or downloaded. It is the one that decides whether
the triage model is safe to use on real Bangladeshi patients, and it is the only
item on this list that requires a hospital to say yes.

## Collecting the Bangla corpus

The realistic route is a partner hospital's outpatient department:

1. At registration, record the complaint **in the patient's own words** — no
   rewriting into clinical language, no correcting spelling. Romanised Bangla
   and code-switching are the signal, not noise.
2. The attending doctor ticks the urgency band they actually assigned, and the
   final diagnosis once known.
3. De-identify before anything leaves the hospital.

3,000–5,000 rows is enough to retire the synthetic corpus. At 30 patients a day
in one busy OPD, that is roughly four to six months.

## Attribution required

Anything adopted must carry its licence:

- Healthsites — ODbL, credit OpenStreetMap contributors
- Mendeley datasets — CC BY 4.0, cite the paper listed on each record
- Kaggle datasets — check each dataset's stated licence
- DGHS / DGDA — government publications, cite the directorate

## Before any real patient data is touched

1. Written ethics approval from the partner institution
2. De-identification before the data leaves the hospital: no name, no phone,
   no address below district, date of birth reduced to age in years
3. A data-sharing agreement stating retention period and permitted use
4. Patient consent covering secondary use for model development
